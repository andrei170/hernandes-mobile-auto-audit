Finding 1 — The Address Is Doing Brand Damage
For a business built on trust and a stranger letting you into their driveway
Your address today hernandesautorepair.webzum.com What it should be hernandesautorepair.com

A customer about to hand their car keys to a mobile mechanic is asking themselves one question: is this a real business or somebody with a toolbox? A free subdomain answers that question badly, and it answers it before they have read a single word.

Three practical consequences beyond how it looks:

  • You cannot have a real email address. There is none published anywhere — and there cannot be one on a domain you do not own. So every customer contact is a phone call.
  • You do not own your own audience. If webzum changes its terms, its pricing or disappears, the address on your van, your invoices and your Google listing stops working.
  • It undercuts the BBB A+. You have held that since October 2019 and it is a genuinely strong signal — sitting on an address that says the opposite.

A domain costs about fifteen dollars a year. It is the cheapest item in this document and the one that changes the brand most.

Finding 2 — It Is One Page Wearing A Four-Page Menu
We followed every link on the site
Menu says Home · Our Services · About Us · Contact Pages that exist 1 Everything else anchor links to sections of the same page

That is not dishonest and plenty of small sites work this way — but it caps you in two ways that matter. Google has one page to rank instead of four, so “mobile mechanic near me”, “mobile brake repair San Diego” and “car diagnostics at home” all compete for the same single page rather than each having one built for them.

And it means there is no room to actually sell any individual service. Brakes, diagnostics and engine work each get one paragraph. For a customer trying to work out whether you can fix their specific problem, one paragraph is not enough to pick up the phone.

Finding 3 — Four Photographs
This is the finding that speaks directly to what you asked for

There are four images on the entire website. You said you want it to look better, and this is the largest single reason it does not.

In mobile auto repair the photographs are the brand. There is no shop for anyone to look at, no reception, no sign on a building. The only physical proof you exist is:

Your van, clean, with signage not shown You, in uniform, at a vehicle not shown The mobile unit and its equipment not shown Work in progress in a driveway not shown

A customer choosing between you and a shop is trying to picture what turns up outside their house. Right now you are asking them to imagine it. No amount of design fixes that — it needs an afternoon with a phone camera, and it will do more for the brand than the layout will.

Finding 4 — No Way To Reach You Except A Phone Call
Read from your served page source
Contact forms 0 Form input fields 0 Email address none published Tap-to-call number 619-871-9186 — works correctly Analytics (GA4) ✗ none

Your phone link is right, and for an emergency breakdown that is exactly what is needed. But not everybody is ready to talk. Somebody comparing three mechanics at ten at night, or a fleet manager who needs to send you five vehicle details, has no way to reach you at all.

And with no analytics of any kind, there is no record of how many people visited and left without calling. That number is invisible today, which is why it never gets fixed.

What Is Genuinely Good — And Should Survive The Rebuild
More of this is right than the grade suggests
  • Your title tag is properly written. “Hernandes Mobile Auto Repair | Mobile Auto Repair Service in San Diego, CA” — service and city. Most sites we audit have nothing this good.
  • Your meta description is written too, and names same-day service, the BBB A+ and the phone number. That is a real piece of work someone did properly.
  • Tap-to-call works on mobile, first tap — and for a breakdown that is the single most important thing on the page.
  • BBB Accredited with an A+ since October 2019. In mobile auto repair, an industry with a trust problem, this is your strongest asset.
  • You are named on the site. “When you call, you speak directly with Israel Hernandes, the owner and lead technician.” Owner-operated, stated plainly — that sells against a chain.
  • Spanish available, which in San Diego is a commercial advantage and should be far more prominent.
  • The page is light at roughly 43KB and loads instantly. Protect that in any rebuild rather than burying it under a heavy template.

This is not a bad website that needs replacing. It is a decent set of foundations on a rented address with no photographs.

One Thing We Checked And Did Not Find
Because in auto repair it is the credential that matters most

We searched the whole page for ASE certification and found no mention of it. In American auto repair that is the qualification customers know by name and look for — more so for a mobile mechanic, where there is no shop to walk into and judge.

We are not assuming you do not hold it. If you or your technicians are ASE certified, publishing that is the single highest-value trust change available to you and it costs nothing. If you are not, tell us on the call and we will build the trust case around the BBB rating and your work instead, rather than claiming something you cannot evidence.

Why A Website Now, With The Diary Full, Is The Right Call
You said you want the site, not advertising. We agree.

Most agencies would try to sell ads to a man who is already full. That would be bad advice — spending to generate work you cannot take wastes the money and annoys the customers you cannot reach.

  • Building while you are busy means it gets done properly — real photographs of your van and your work, your input, no rush.
  • Analytics starts collecting immediately, so when you do want more work you will have months of real data instead of starting blind.
  • Owning the domain compounds. Every month on a rented address is a month of reputation being built on ground you do not control.

We would not sell you advertising today, and we will say so on the call.
